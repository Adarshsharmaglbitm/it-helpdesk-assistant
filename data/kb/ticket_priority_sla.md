# Raising Tickets and Support Response Times

## How to get help
- **Self-service portal** (`support.company.internal`): The fastest way for most requests and non-urgent issues. Choose the right category so the ticket goes straight to the correct team.
- **IT Service Desk phone line**: For urgent issues, security incidents, and when you cannot access the portal. Available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.
- **IT walk-up desk**: On each office's ground floor, open Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 18:00, for hardware collection and in-person help.

## Ticket priorities and response times
| Priority | Meaning | Example | First response | Target resolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 Critical | Business stopped for many users, or a security incident | Email down company-wide, clicked phishing link | 15 minutes | 4 hours |
| P2 High | One team blocked, or a key user fully blocked | Department cannot access a core app | 1 hour | 8 business hours |
| P3 Medium | One user affected, workaround exists | Printer not working, slow laptop | 4 business hours | 3 business days |
| P4 Low | Requests and questions | New software, peripheral request | 1 business day | 5 business days |

The Service Desk may change the priority you choose based on actual impact.

## Writing a good ticket
Include what you were trying to do, the exact error message or a screenshot, when it started, what you already tried, and how many people are affected. Clear tickets are resolved faster.

## Escalation
If a ticket has passed its target resolution time with no update, reply to the ticket email asking for escalation, or call the Service Desk and quote the ticket number.
