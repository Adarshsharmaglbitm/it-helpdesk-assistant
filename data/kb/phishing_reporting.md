# Reporting Phishing and Security Incidents

## Recognizing phishing
Phishing emails try to trick you into clicking a link, opening an attachment, or sharing credentials. Common warning signs:
- Urgent language, such as "your account will be closed today".
- A sender address that does not match the company name it claims to be from.
- Requests for passwords, MFA codes, gift cards, or payments.
- Links whose real address (shown when you hover over them) differs from the text.
- Unexpected attachments, especially zipped files or files asking you to enable macros.

## How to report a suspicious email
Use the **Report Phishing** button in the Outlook toolbar (desktop, web, and mobile). This sends the email to the Security team and removes it from your inbox. Do not forward suspicious emails to colleagues to ask whether they are safe.

## If you clicked a link or entered your password
Act quickly; minutes matter:
1. Disconnect from the network (turn off Wi-Fi or unplug the cable) if you opened an attachment.
2. Change your password immediately using the self-service password portal from a different device.
3. Call the IT Service Desk security hotline and report it as a **P1 security incident**.
4. Do not delete the email, because the Security team needs it for investigation.

You will not be punished for reporting a mistake. Reporting quickly is what limits the damage.

## Lost or stolen devices
A lost or stolen laptop or phone is also a security incident. Report it within 1 hour by calling the IT Service Desk so the device can be locked and wiped remotely. See the article on hardware replacement for getting a new device.
